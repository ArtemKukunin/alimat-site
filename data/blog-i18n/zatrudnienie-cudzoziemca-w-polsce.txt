<p><strong>Employing foreigners in Poland: why it is an increasingly popular solution</strong></p>
<p>For many years Poland has been an attractive labour market for foreigners. We welcome workers from many countries because we can offer <strong>better employment conditions</strong> than many other European countries. For some, the advantage is <strong>competitive pay</strong>; for others, it is <strong>stability and quality of life in Poland</strong>.</p>
<p>Increasingly, <strong>staff shortages in specific industries</strong> can be filled effectively by experienced workers from abroad. However, it is worth remembering that <strong>the process of hiring a foreigner</strong> can be more complex than hiring a Polish citizen.</p>
<p>In this article we explain step by step <strong>how hiring a foreigner in Poland works</strong>, which <strong>documents are required</strong> and how to ensure <strong>legal work and residence</strong>.</p>
<hr><p><strong>Who counts as a foreigner, and what are their rights to work in Poland?</strong></p>
<p>Legally, a <strong>foreigner</strong> is anyone who does not hold Polish citizenship. However, how they can work legally depends on which country they come from.</p>
<p>There are two main groups of foreigners:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Citizens of the European Union, the EEA and Switzerland</strong> — can work in Poland without a permit.</li>
<li><strong>Citizens of non-EU countries</strong> — must have an appropriate <strong>work permit</strong> or a <strong>declaration on entrusting work</strong> (oświadczenie o powierzeniu wykonywania pracy).</li>
</ul>
<p>There are also <strong>exceptions</strong> that exempt people from the permit requirement. These include holders of the <strong>Pole’s Card (Karta Polaka)</strong>, people with <strong>refugee status</strong> and <strong>students studying in Poland</strong>.</p>
<hr><p><strong>Hiring a foreigner in Poland step by step</strong></p>
<h3><strong>1. Checking the legality of stay</strong></h3>
<p>The basis of employment is the foreigner’s <strong>legal stay</strong> in Poland. The employer should check on what basis the foreigner is staying in the country — it may be a <strong>visa, a residence card</strong> or <strong>visa-free travel</strong>.</p>
<p>Note that <strong>not every visa allows you to work</strong> — for example, a student or tourist visa does not give that right.</p>
<hr><h3><strong>2. Choosing the right employment procedure</strong></h3>
<p>The type of procedure depends on the foreigner’s country of origin and the kind of work. The most common forms are:</p>
<ul><li><strong>A declaration on entrusting work (oświadczenie)</strong> — the simplest procedure, for citizens of countries including Ukraine, Belarus, Georgia, Moldova and Armenia.</li>
<li><strong>A work permit (type A, B, C, D or E)</strong> — issued by the voivode depending on the type and length of employment.</li>
<li><strong>A residence and work permit</strong> — for people already in Poland who want to continue working legally.</li>
</ul>
<hr><h3><strong>3. Documents required to hire a foreigner</strong></h3>
<p>To apply for a declaration or permit, you need to prepare a full set of documents, including:</p>

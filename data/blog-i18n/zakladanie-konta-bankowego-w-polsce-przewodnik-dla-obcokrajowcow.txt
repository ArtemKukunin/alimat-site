<h2><strong>Who can open a bank account in Poland?</strong></h2>
<p>Foreigners from many countries can open bank accounts in Poland, but the requirements may vary depending on where you come from and your legal status. Broadly speaking, there are two main groups:</p>
<ul><li><strong>EU citizens:</strong> the process is somewhat simpler, and banks usually only ask for an identity document and an address in Poland.</li>
<li><strong>Non-EU citizens:</strong> banks may ask for additional documents, such as a residence card, a PESEL number or a certificate of employment.</li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>Documents needed to open an account</strong></h2>
<p>Depending on the bank and your status as a foreigner, these are the documents most often required:</p>
<ul><li>Passport or ID card — the basic document for verifying your identity.</li>
<li>Residence card (karta pobytu) — often required for people from outside the European Union.</li>
<li>PESEL — a PESEL number may be required, although some banks let you open an account without one.</li>
<li>Address — a document confirming where you live in Poland, such as a tenancy agreement.</li>
<li>Certificate of employment or employment contract — proof that you have a source of income.</li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>A bank account without a PESEL — is it possible?</strong></h2>
<p>Yes, some banks let you open an account without a PESEL number. In that case, the key documents are your passport and your address in Poland. It is still worth getting a PESEL, though, as it makes many administrative procedures in Poland easier.</p>
<h2><strong>Benefits of having a bank account in Poland</strong></h2>
<p>A bank account in Poland brings many benefits, especially if you are planning a longer stay or work. Here are some of them:</p>
<ul><li>Receiving your pay — your employer will transfer your salary directly into your account.</li>
<li>Card payments — most shops and services in Poland prefer card payments, which is more convenient than using cash.</li>
<li>Savings — an account lets you put money aside for the future, and some banks offer attractive savings terms.</li>
